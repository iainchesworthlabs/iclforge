    An ESP32-P4 sink¶

    hearth_sink runs on the ESP32-P4 as the best of the sink family: the part with the room to decode E-AC-3 7.1.4 with its tools on, and AC-4 to 5.1, in real time. It has one I2S controller whose TDM frame holds 512 bits, which reaches sixteen 32-bit channels into both DACs of the planned pair from one line, and, on a board with a panel, it draws the words of a karaoke song as they are sung.

    Status as of 2026-10-20: the v1.x board plays; the v3.x kit shows the screen, takes a tap, plays through 16 TDM slots, plays a karaoke song with its words and decodes AC-4 5.1; the clocks on the pins are not measured, and the glass costs the heaviest stream a tenth

    The sink runs on a DFRobot FireBeetle 2 (chip revision v1.3), stereo, over Wi-Fi hosted on its onboard ESP32-C6. The v3.x kit arrived on 2026-10-20 and the v3.x image ran on it the same day: it boots on a v3.2 chip at 400 MHz, joins Wi-Fi, is found by Music Assistant, draws the idle and the lyric screens on the panel, answers a tap on every button of the controls sheet, and plays a 5.1 stream through the full sixteen 32-bit TDM slots in real time with no underrun. A karaoke song with word-timed lyrics then played for 99 s with no underrun or late chunk, the words following it on the glass, and the owner, who watched and used the Adjust sheet, said it appeared and looked fine. The glass takes decode time: its scan-out costs the decoder 1.3% (AC-3), 6% (E-AC-3) and about 10% (AC-4 5.1), and with the song filling the screen the light streams' decode time moves by 5 to 12% (under 1% of a frame). The glitches the first measurement put on the glass are not its: about one play in five reaches the kit with its first chunk 300 to 600 ms late, with the glass, without it and at 40 Hz, over Wi-Fi and (more often) over wired, and those plays lose the start of their first second: lwIP's six-entry TCP receive mailbox refused the first segments; PR #1610 makes it 32, and none of 40 plays had a late first chunk (the trace). Not checked on the kit: the TDM clocks on the pins (it needs a scope) and tearing beyond the owner's look. The board suite is played on it, with a baseline. Its RJ45 port works beside the Wi-Fi (wired): the sink answers on it, plays Sendspin through it, takes an update over it, falls back to Wi-Fi when the Ethernet cable is pulled and returns to wired when it is put back, so the kit can run on a PoE splitter; the splitter itself is not yet tried. planning/karaoke-p4x-panel.md has the plan, the first run and the list of what it runs next.

    Pairing, groups and firmware updates work the same as on the S3; see An ESP32-S3 sink and Sink firmware. This page covers what differs on the P4.

    Two chips, two images¶

    The P4 has had two silicon generations, and their firmware images are not interchangeable (Espressif's v3.x user guide, section 1.1): an image for one refuses to start on the other. A release carries one for each.

    GET /hardware tells which a board is, and ota.py chooses the image from it, so a board updated over its network gets the right one (Sink firmware).

    Build and flash¶

    From firmware/hearth-sink, in an ESP-IDF v6.1 terminal. A P4 is not built with set-target and plain defaults: say the target and the overlays.

    Chip revision v1.x (the FireBeetle 2, stereo, no screen):

    idf.py -DIDF_TARGET=esp32p4 "-DSDKCONFIG_DEFAULTS=sdkconfig.defaults;sdkconfig.hw;sdkconfig.p4;sdkconfig.sendspin;sdkconfig.ac4" build
idf.py -p PORT flash monitor

    Chip revision v3.x with the 7-inch panel (the image CI publishes as hearth-sink-esp32p4-rev3):

    idf.py -DIDF_TARGET=esp32p4 "-DSDKCONFIG_DEFAULTS=sdkconfig.defaults;sdkconfig.hw;sdkconfig.p4;sdkconfig.p4-rev3;sdkconfig.sendspin;sdkconfig.ac4;sdkconfig.panel;sdkconfig.panel-dsi" build

    A board with no panel, to see the screen (any P4; the picture is read back over HTTP, below):

    idf.py -DIDF_TARGET=esp32p4 "-DSDKCONFIG_DEFAULTS=sdkconfig.defaults;sdkconfig.hw;sdkconfig.p4;sdkconfig.sendspin;sdkconfig.panel;sdkconfig.panel-null" build
    File What it sets
    sdkconfig.p4 The P4 itself: the wide I2S sink, PSRAM, the code run from PSRAM, the Wi-Fi buffers and the DMA queue, with the reasons for each. Written for v1.x silicon
    sdkconfig.p4-rev3 After sdkconfig.p4: chip revisions v3.1 to v3.99, 400 MHz, and the bootloader at the Warn log level (a v3.x bootloader is 25,152 bytes at Info against the 24,576-byte window; 22,448 at Warn)
    sdkconfig.panel LVGL's heap in PSRAM, TrueType fonts, right-to-left text
    sdkconfig.panel-null / sdkconfig.panel-dsi The frame buffer with no panel, or the 7-inch MIPI DSI panel

    AC-4 is sdkconfig.ac4, in both release profiles: since the fold of p4-rev1-ac4 into p4-rev1 and, on 2026-10-29, into p4-rev3, an image of either revision decodes AC-3, E-AC-3 and AC-4. The v3.x image keeps the decode task's stack of an HTTP play in PSRAM (sdkconfig.p4-rev3): the screen leaves it 74 KB of internal RAM at the start of a play, and an AC-4 5.1 play leaves 8 to 10 KB of it at its tightest (planning/ac4-in-release-images.md; ESP32-P4 platform page).

    On the v1.x board¶

    It plays AC-3 and E-AC-3 onto 2.0 in a paired Sendspin session, and decodes AC-4 to 5.1 in real time (ESP32-P4). TDM does not open above two channels on this silicon: the controller has no PLL clock source before v3, and the audio PLL it falls back to tops out at 125 MHz, short of the ~147 MHz a 512-bit frame needs at 48 kHz. That is a limit of the chip, and GET /hardware says so on such a board.

    Karaoke on the v1.x board¶

    The board reproduces AC-3 and E-AC-3 karaoke itself at its 2.0 layout, which is the only one it can open. Hearth's live karaoke cases at ICLFORGE_HEARTH_LIVE_KARAOKE_LAYOUT=2.0, on a v1.3 board booted from its first slot on 2026-10-13, found the board's output levels within 0.2 dB of the host's decode for AC-3 and within 0.7 dB for E-AC-3, for as coded, aware, and capable with a vocal, with none, and with the melody and a vocal moved, with the layout, routing, trim, delay and crossover settings pushed as well. At 2.0 the aware reproduction is the Lo/Ro fold as coded already is, so the two read alike. The 3.0 layout the case uses by default needs three slots and cannot open here; it is not measured on a v3.x chip.

    One earlier run on the same image, started from the board's second slot straight after an update restart, played nothing and logged burst player: a stream control message did not fit the ring (the player's ring was not draining). It did not repeat in eight runs after a clean start from the first slot, and its cause is not known.

    On the v3.x kit¶

    Run on a v3.2 chip and a panel; the pins' clocks are not measured

    What follows was built from ESP-IDF's headers, the v3.x user guide and the vendor drivers' own values, and the first run on the kit (2026-10-20) corrected it in four places: a crash in the DSI code, the touch controller's address (0x14), its axes (turned half a turn from the display's) and how often it must be read, all fixed. A line below that says what the kit did was seen on it; one that says what the chip should do was not.

    What changes for the audio¶

    • 400 MHz, and PSRAM at 200 MHz by ESP-IDF's default for this chip. The decoders do not scale with the clock: on the kit, with no panel, the decoder alone takes 10% less time than on the v1.3 board for AC-3 and 17% less for AC-4 (11% more clock), and a Sendspin burst at the same layout (2.0) takes 3 to 6% less. A burst used to cost about 176 µs for each output of the layout (the renderer, the patch, the trims, the meters and the playout's copy) whether or not the stream filled it, and the kit's player starts at 9.1.6, sixteen outputs, where the v1.3 board's sink has two: the kit's first Sendspin figures were 13% to 100% above the v1.3 board's for that reason. The player now does that work for the outputs that carry audio only, and hands the DAC zeros for the rest: an AC-3 2.0 stream takes 2.4 ms a burst at 2.0, 5.1 and 9.1.6 alike (2.6, 3.3 and 5.2 ms before), and an AC-4 5.1 stream 29.9 ms at 9.1.6 (33.4 before). The kit's figures are in Memory and time.
    • TDM opens: the i2s_wide sink takes the 160 MHz PLL (I2S_CLK_SRC_PLL_160M) as the clock source on this silicon, which the 16 × 32-bit frame needs (a 147 MHz source at least). The source is chosen when the image is built, because the two revisions are two images. On the kit, a 5.1 stream rendered onto the layout 9.1.6 opened sixteen 32-bit slots and played for 30 s in real time with no underrun, 30,336 ms of audio in 30,185 ms of wall clock, the six coded channels in their slots and the other ten at zero. Whether the clocks leave the pins at 24.576 MHz (BCLK) and 48 kHz (WS) is not measured: it needs a scope or a logic analyser.
    • The DACs are not wired and no firmware programs an ES9080 over I2C: the board plays to a frame whose unused slots are zero, which is what the ES9080 contract is (sink tiers).

    The screen¶

    The board shows the lyrics of the item playing, against the sound the board is playing: the server sends the document and where in it the sound is, and the board follows on its own clock, which knows its own output delay. The same lyric file and the same karaoke stream Hearth plays on a desktop (Karaoke).

    One colour for a word sung, one fill for the word being sung, and one for a word not yet sung, as on the desktop, with one cue that is not colour (the underline), so that no one has to tell the colours apart. The picture above and the others on this page are drawn by the firmware's own screen code (firmware/hearth-sink/main/panel/lvgl/lyric_screen.cpp) and rendered by a tool on a computer (below).

Which vocals sound and the reproduction are the server's settings and are shown, not changed, until the role has a way to carry a request back.

    Adjust and Preferences¶

    The footer has two buttons, Preferences and Adjust (Adjust is where it always was, at the right). One is for what a person does while a song is on, the other for what they choose once and leave.

    Adjust keeps the two tweaks of the lyric screen: the lyric delay (50 ms steps, up to 1,000 ms either way) and the text size (Smaller, Normal or Larger). It is 250 px tall on the kit's panel (it was 352 when it also held the screen for a song with no lyrics and its dim time) and puts itself away after eight seconds untouched.

    Preferences is a full-screen, scrollable list of everything that is chosen once and left, grouped Display, Sound, Network, Time, Updates and System (a group with nothing in it is not drawn), with Close at the top right. It keeps the screen lit while it is open, puts itself away after a minute untouched, and is built when it opens and deleted when it closes, so the lyric screen is as it was with it shut. It holds what the screen shows while a song has no lyrics and when it dims (the two settings that were in Adjust), the screen's brightness, how the clock view writes the time, the night schedule and the time zone, then the Sound group (the test tone through each speaker, and the level and distance of each speaker on the Speakers sheet), then the Network and System groups: wired or Wi-Fi, the player's name, About, the paired servers, Restart, Reset preferences and Factory reset (On the screen). The technical settings (slot width, the second I2S line, the time server) stay in the developer view and the routes.

    The brightness is a stepper (every tenth from 10% to 100%), the clock's hours and seconds are a choice and a switch, and the night schedule is a switch, a button that opens an editor for its two times, and a switch for whether it darkens the idle screen too. A value that is chosen in a list or a pair of times is a button that opens an editor over the sheet, with a Done of its own:

    Each time steps a quarter of an hour round the clock (22:00 up is 22:15, and 00:00 down is 23:45); a button held keeps stepping, ten times a second, and after eight steps moves an hour at a time. The pair is written half a second after the last step, so that a held button is one write and not forty. The editor says what the two mean (Dark from 22:00 until 07:00, through midnight, by the board's own time. A touch lights the screen for 30 seconds.), that the schedule is switched off when it is, and, for the same time twice, that the screen is never dark.

    The time zone is the same picker as the web page's: the regions of the zone table (Africa to Pacific, and No zone (UTC) above them), then the places in one, opening at the region of the zone already chosen with that place in view, so a person who wants the city next to the board's own does not scroll for it. A tap on a place chooses it at once; the editor says Now: Melbourne (Australia), and Regions (a chevron on a narrow bar) goes back up.

    One product base, many boards. What a board can do is data, not an #ifdef and not "is this the P4X". iclforge::BoardCaps (libs/device/include/iclforge/board_caps.hpp) describes it: the display (how it is attached, its size, whether it has touch and a backlight), the sink (the bits in a TDM frame, whether a second line is possible, whether the chip can open a TDM frame at all, and the slot width and second line in force), the networks, whether the board keeps the time, the object cap, and the chip's own facts (HardwareFacts, which GET /hardware reports). The firmware fills it from the build and the hardware (main/board_caps.cpp), and board_variants.hpp has every board the product knows and a few it does not (an ESP32-S3 with a 320 by 240 SPI panel, an ESP32-C3 with a panel that has no touch) for the host tests to walk.

    The preference registry (libs/device/include/iclforge/preferences.hpp) is the list the screen, GET /preferences and the web page all draw from. An entry has an id, a group, a label, a kind (choice, stepper, toggle, text, action, info, list or sheet: a row that says what is set and opens an editor of its own), whether a change applies live, at the next play or after a restart, the route that sets the same value, its choices, what Reset preferences does with it (on_reset: put back to its default word, or keep; an entry that says neither fails a host test), and availability(caps), once for the entry and once for each choice: whether this board can do it and, if not, why. The values are read and set through a typed Backend that the firmware implements once over the settings_* functions (main/preferences.cpp), so the screen, the routes and the host tests go through the same code.

    A preference the board cannot support is shown, greyed, with the reason, and never hidden. On a board whose screen has no touch, the Dark view (which a touch lights) and the dim time (which a touch wakes) are greyed, and the sheet says why:

    A tap on a greyed control does nothing, and prefs::set_value() refuses it before the board is asked. The host tests (libs/device/tests/test_preferences.cpp) check every entry against every known board: a reason wherever something is greyed, and a default that is never a choice the board cannot make.

    Any size of screen. The sizes are the display's, not 1024 by 600's. libs/device/include/iclforge/screen_metrics.hpp derives the margins, the gaps, the type and the height of a button from the display's shorter side (host tested for every size), and the footer and both sheets are laid out from them, so the kit's own sizes are as they were:

    320 by 240 10 16 48 / 48 170 px

    The fingertip. A control that is touched is at least 48 px on both sides, with at least 6 px to the next one: 48 px is 7.2 mm on the kit's 7-inch 1024 by 600 panel (about 6.7 px a millimetre) and 7.4 mm on a 2.4-inch 320 by 240 module, over the 7 mm that Apple and Google give as the least a finger hits reliably (44 pt, 48 dp). The preview tool (below) draws Adjust and Preferences at the four sizes above and fails the run if text is cut off, a control sticks out of what holds it or off the screen, a touch target is under 48 px or closer than 6 px to another, a tap does not do what it says, a greyed choice answers a tap, or a list longer than the glass does not scroll. A display backend for a chip other than the P4 is not written (no such panel is on the bench): panel/lvgl/display.hpp is the seam one would fill, and everything above it takes its size from the display.

    Adding a preference is a registry entry (id, group, label, choices and their availability rules), a getter and a setter in Backend (a pure virtual, so a firmware that forgot the setter does not build), the same pair in the host tests' fake, and the firmware's implementation; only a control that is not one of the five kinds needs an editor, and it names one (Pref::editor: a pair of times, a place in a list) which panel/lvgl/prefs_editors.cpp draws. There are no bespoke buttons in lyric_screen.cpp.

    A tap's setter runs on the panel's own task, which holds the panel's lock (a std::mutex, not recursive) for the frame that reads the tap, so no panel_set_* may take that lock or touch the glass: a tap on Brightness - or + once hung the screen for good (the first run on the kit, 2026-10-26) because the brightness setter did both. What the screen follows is iclforge::panelfollow::PanelFollow (libs/device/include/iclforge/panel_follow.hpp): atomics only, the brightness only marks the backlight stale, and the panel's loop gives the glass the level in its next cycle (within a tenth of a second). test_panel_follow.cpp holds a lock across every setter, and reads panel.cpp to fail if a panel_set_* mentions a lock, a wait or the glass.

    A song with no lyrics¶

    Most of what is played has none. While a stream plays and the board holds no lyrics, the panel shows the speakers: the room the layout in force describes, seen from above with the front at the top. Ear-level speakers are circles, height speakers rounded squares, the LFE a wide box along the bottom, and each speaker glows with its own level, from the board's own measure of the last 100 ms. A speaker with nothing on it is a hollow outline, so a dead output can be seen. The line above says what arrives and what it is played on (Dolby Digital Plus 5.1 to 7.1.4, PCM to 2.0).

    It is meant to be left on for hours in a dim room: the glow moves in a few steps, and is never brighter than the accent at 55%. After five minutes with nothing touched (the time is a Preference, or PUT /dim-after: never, or whole minutes from 1 to 240) the backlight goes to 4% and a black screen with one line is over everything; any tap brings the speakers back, and the tap does not also press what is under it. Only this screen dims: the lyric screens and the idle screen do not.

    How the speakers follow the sound (the plan is planning/p4x-speakers-options.md). The screen reads the level of every 100 ms window the player measures (a lock and two short arrays, BurstPlayer::levels(); the heading and the title are still read five times a second), where it once read every other one. A level is judged against the loudest the room has lately been (it rises at once and falls 2 dB a second, and is never lower than -48 dB of full scale), so that the same music shows the same at any volume: the player's volume is applied before the meter, and against full scale the glow would shrink with the knob. The core of a speaker is six steps over 30 dB (6 dB a step) and the halo, from the peak, two. The level rises at once and falls at 60 dB a second, so a hit is a step down in the window after the next, and a step changes only when the level is 0.4 of a step past its edge (0.7 for the halo), so that a level on a boundary does not flicker. A speaker with nothing on it is still a hollow outline. These are the constants of libs/render/include/iclforge/render/room_plan.hpp, chosen over a synthetic groove in 2.0, 5.1 and 7.1.4 (firmware/hearth-sink/preview/speakers_options/tune.cpp): 72% of the onsets of 5 dB or more showed as a step up in the same window (the 5 Hz glow before it: 3 to 26%), 3 to 5% of steady windows moved a step, and the view flushed 18 000 pixels a second at 2.0, 56 000 at 5.1, 82 000 at 7.1.4 and 101 000 at 9.1.6 (the lyric screen's song filling: 192 000). The pixels are what the view costs; what they cost a stream on the kit (the first design, at about 45 000 a second at 5.1, cost a burst 0.4%, below) is still to be measured for these constants.

    The speakers' style. glow (the default) is the above. pulse makes the filled circle of each speaker grow and shrink with its level, a little: eight sizes between 88% and 112% of the plan's, moved by at most three in a window, held until the level is a quarter size past an edge, and a speaker 18 dB or more under the room's loudest (or silent) stays at its smallest and does not move. The halo is not resized, so a size change redraws the circle's rectangle and nothing else. It needs no meter and no seam the glow does not have. It is set in Preferences (Display) or with PUT /speakers-style; the value is kept on the board as np_style (0 glow, 1 pulse; never renumbered).

    Both styles stay: the owner, 2026-10-25, saw the glow, as it was and as it became, beside the pulse at plus and minus 12% (the bound is kPulseReach), and kept both as a choice, with the glow the default.

    Which screen shows is the owner's: speakers (the default), title, clock (below), dark, or off, which is the old sentence. The plan, with how each was chosen and built, is (planning/p4x-now-playing.md).

    The title card. The board lists metadata@v1 in its hello, which a board with a screen does and one without does not, so a server that has the role tells it what is playing: the title, the artist, the album and its year, and where in the song it is. The card is those, and nothing the server did not say: the title in 72 down to 34 px by its length (the lyric line's rule, and the same Smaller and Larger steps), in two rows at most with three dots after them; the artist under it in 36 px, the album and year in 24 px muted; the song's time at the top right and a progress line under the header, as the lyric screens have them, where the server gave a progress and the board's clock has converged.

    The Meters view. A choice of the screen without lyrics (meters, stored as np_view 5, never renumbered; Preferences, Display, Screen without lyrics, or PUT /now-playing): an instrument rather than a picture of the room. The top of the screen is a third-octave spectrum of the main mix (31 bands from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, 0 dB a full-scale sine, 60 dB of range) with a dB scale, a peak tick that holds one second and then falls, and the colours of a level meter: green, then amber from -30 dB and red from -15 dB. Under it are the room's channels, drawn the way the size of the screen and the number of outputs ask for (iclforge/meters_view.hpp, representation_for):

    Screen width A 2.0 room More than two outputs
    under 400 px the pair, L and R, with a peak tick each the pair (the stereo downmix of the room)
    400 to 799 px the pair one meter for each of L, C, R, LFE, Ls, Rs (the surround sum)
    800 px and wider the pair every output, in groups: Front, Surround, Rear, Height, LFE

    The bars are green, amber from -18 dB and red from -6 dB, with a peak tick that holds 1.5 s. The spectrum has one bar a band on the wide screen, two bands a bar on the middle one and three (an octave) on the small one. The Meters view needs a screen of at least 240 by 240 and the Sendspin player, because the player is where the sound is: on a board without either, the choice is shown greyed with the reason and PUT /now-playing answers 409. While Meters shows, Speakers style does nothing (the glow and pulse are the speakers' view) and is greyed too.

    What it draws comes from the player, not the glass: after the output stage has set its gain, BurstPlayer hands each block of the frames about to be played to a BlockTap (iclforge/block_tap.hpp), one pointer read when nothing is listening. MeterTap (iclforge/meters_tap.hpp) keeps a mono mix of the outputs in a ring of 340 ms and, for each output, the largest sample in each 50 ms; the screen takes the mix, analyses it (iclforge/dsp/spectrum.hpp: a 2048-point transform of the signal at 12 kHz for the 17 bands up to 800 Hz and a 1024-point one at 48 kHz above it, so that a low band is not smeared over a fifth of a second and a high one is not slow), averages the power over 80 ms, and lets the bars fall at 30 dB a second (meters 24). It does this twenty times a second whatever the frame rate. The spectrum, the channel bars and the pair of meters are each one canvas, redrawn only where a bar or a tick moved, in a few rectangles (one for each run of neighbouring bars that move alike), so an update is a handful of invalid areas and not a hundred (a rectangle for each part of each bar, which the kit measured at 80 to 290 ms a refresh). When the screen is dimmed or another view is chosen the tap is removed, so a board that is not showing meters pays nothing for them. Everything the view draws, and the analysis's tables (about 63 KB with the transform's work buffers, in PSRAM), exist only in an image with a panel; the C6 and S3 images have the tap's interface and nothing else. How fast it runs on the kit, and the protocol that measures it, are in (planning/p4x-meters-view.md).

    What the Meters view costs, and the rate it holds. GET /panel-stats (port 80, on a board with a screen) says what the panel costs: LVGL's refreshes (the start of one to its end, the flush to the glass included), the panel task's stack never-free minimum, the PSRAM and internal heap now and at their lowest, and, once the view has been opened, the Meters view's own figures since it opened: the rate it holds, how long an update's analysis, widgets and render take (last, mean, largest), the updates that were late or missed, and what the player's tap costs the audio path for a burst of 1,536 frames. The developer view shows the same in its PERFORMANCE section. The view runs at twenty updates a second when the panel's task can keep up and ten when it cannot (iclforge/meters_governor.hpp): over the last 20 updates, an average cost above 70 % of the 50 ms step, or 4 updates started more than 15 ms later than the loop could have started them, drops it to ten; it returns to twenty only after 10 s at ten with the cost under 60 % and nothing late, so that it does not flip; the first two seconds of a view are not judged (its first draw and the cold code are not what it costs). The target is 20 Hz if possible, else a steady 10 Hz (no more than 1 % of the steps without an update, an update under half of its step), and the kit protocol says which room holds which. The audio comes first: while the decoder is busy (a mean burst of 14 ms or more of its 32: a 7.1.4 object stream, AC-4; not a 5.1 E-AC-3 room) the view is held at ten, since the drawing and the analysis beside the decoder cost it 1.5 to 2 ms a burst in those rooms (ICLFORGE_EXAMPLE_PANEL_YIELD, default 1; 2 also keeps the panel's task on the decode core, and 0 turns it off). How each room holds up on the kit, and the protocol that measures it, are in (planning/p4x-meters-view.md).

While the speakers or the card show, the footer says what the stream is and who sends it, in the most that fits: Decoded on this board - 768 kb/s - from Music Assistant, then Decoded here - 768 kb/s - from Music Assistant, then the same without the server, then the figure alone. The server is the name in /status server without the id in brackets (Music Assistant (d5369777-music-assistant) is Music Assistant); the developer view keeps the whole string.

What the store keeps is small and fixed (a title of 256 bytes, an artist or an album of 160, cut at a character and not in the middle of one), in strings that keep their capacity, so a server's update and the panel's five reads a second allocate nothing from the internal heap an AC-4 decoder leaves little of.     The clock¶

    The time of day, for a room where the sound is the thing and the panel is a glance: the time at 200 px, the date under it, and nothing else, in the muted tone (the time at about 80% of it and the date at about 60%, so that neither is the brightest thing in a dim room). By default it is in 24 hours and shows no seconds, so what the screen redraws is the time's rectangle once a minute (the preview counts 224,000 pixels, against none for a second that passes), and the line over it still says what the stream is (Dolby Digital Plus 5.1 to 5.1).

    A person can have 12 hours (9:47, with a small PM beside it; midnight is 12:05 AM) and the seconds (small beside the digits, :12), in any combination: PUT /clock-format takes 24, 12, 24 seconds or 12 seconds. The small text is a label of its own, so a second that passes redraws about 9,600 pixels and not the digits' 300,000, and with the seconds off a second redraws nothing.

    The time is the board's own, kept with a time server, which every board does. Until the first answer the view draws the heading and nothing else, never a time it made up (and an answer from before 2026 is a server with a wrong clock, and is not taken for the time either).

    The night schedule¶

    Go dark between two times, by the board's own time (the time of day, in its zone). It is off until a person switches it on, and then it is 22:00 to 07:00 until they change the times. While it is in force the no-lyrics screen behaves as the dark view does: dark from the moment it begins to show (a song that starts at 23:00 is dark at once; one already playing at 22:00 goes dark once nothing has touched the glass for 30 seconds), a tap lights it for 30 seconds from the last touch, and the tap that lights it does not also press what is under it. A window that ends before it starts crosses midnight; the same time twice is an empty window (never dark: a whole day is the Dark view's job); a board that has not heard the time yet is lit, never dark by accident; and a zone with daylight saving moves the window with the clock (the night the clocks go forward is eight real hours of dark, the night they go back ten). With the Dark view chosen, or the screen without lyrics Off, the rows say the schedule is not used (The Dark view is dark already: a touch lights it for 30 seconds, as at night.).

    The idle screen is a switch of its own (night-idle, Night covers idle screen), off. Whether the night should darken the screen between two songs as well is the owner's to say; with it on, the idle screen counts as showing while the night is in force and is dark from the moment the night begins, with the same touch. It is not used while the schedule is off, and the row says so.

    Kept as one number. NVS night holds the two times and both switches (from in bits 0 to 10, to in bits 11 to 21, bit 22 on, bit 23 the idle screen), so a power cut cannot leave half a schedule; a number no build wrote is dropped with a line on the console and the Kconfig default (ICLFORGE_EXAMPLE_NIGHT_SCHEDULE, 22:00-07:00 off, and ICLFORGE_EXAMPLE_NIGHT_IDLE) stands. Reset preferences returns it; the time zone it is read in stays. The panel is handed the schedule and the board's local minute of the day (read only while the schedule is on, and nothing until the clock has converged) and lyric_screen.cpp passes both to dim_state(): that is the whole of what the screen does for it. The rule, its words and its changeover cases are host-tested (test_now_view.cpp), and the preview tool drives it on the screen: lit at noon, dark at 23:00, a touch lights it for 30 s, lit at 07:00 and with no time known, and the idle screen dark only when told.

    The time of day¶

    Every ESP board that has a network keeps its time with a time server, by default, from the moment it holds an address: all the hardware (the Wi-Fi boards and the P4X with its wired network), where QEMU's network has nothing to ask and a build with no network has nothing to ask over, so those keep no time (the routes below answer 404). It is lwIP's SNTP client (firmware/hearth-sink/main/clock/sntp/). The panel's clock view draws what it keeps; a board with no screen has it for its page, GET /status and the console. Nothing listens: SNTP is a request the board sends and the answer comes back to it.

    Which server is asked, in this order:

    1. The one a person named (PUT /ntp-server: a host name or an IPv4 address). Naming a server says where the time comes from, so with one named the board asks it and then the router's, and not the pool.
    2. The one the router gave over DHCP (option 42), which is the default: lwIP keeps it when a lease is bound (net/stack.cpp turns that on at boot), so it is there from the first lease, and a lease renewed with another replaces it.
    3. pool.ntp.org, when nobody named a server.

    A lease rewrites lwIP's table of servers when it is bound (the router's takes the first slot and the rest are emptied), so the board looks at the table once a second and puts it back as it should be; the router's server is remembered from the first slot. The first request goes out after a random delay of up to five seconds (RFC 4330 asks it of a device that has just started), and then once an hour. A new address starts the question again at once if no server has answered yet.

    Route Takes Default
    GET and PUT /time-sync on or off on
    GET and PUT /ntp-server a host name or an IPv4 address, or none none: the router's, then the pool
    GET and PUT /timezone a name from GET /time-zones (Australia/Melbourne), a POSIX time zone, or none none: UTC
    GET /time-zones the names PUT /timezone takes, one a line 420 of them

    All three are stored on the board (NVS time_sync, ntp_server, time_zone, and tz_name for the name a person chose) and take effect at once, with no restart. They are on the board's own web page too, in a Time section of Settings: the time the board keeps and how it got it, a checkbox for whether to ask a time server, a region and a city (the page asks the board for its list of zones) with a field for any other zone, and a field for the server (which says, under it, the servers lwIP is set to ask). GET /status has a time object (sync, asking, synced, local, zone, zone_name, zone_place, zone_label, server, servers, answers), and the console says when the board started asking (wall clock: asking for the time: 192.168.1.5 (from DHCP), pool.ntp.org), each answer, and when it stopped. off is for a board that must contact no time server: it stops asking and forgets the time (a clock left running with nothing correcting it is a time the board made up), and a clock view then shows only its heading.

    The time zone is a POSIX string, because the firmware has no zone database: PUT /timezone AEST-10AEDT,M10.1.0,M4.1.0/3 is Sydney, Melbourne and Hobart. Its offset is the hours west of UTC, so an Australian zone has a minus sign; none takes the zone away. A person need not type one. The board carries a table of the zones people choose by region and city, 420 of them in 11,578 bytes of flash, generated from IANA's tzdata (2026e; tools/hearth/gen_time_zones.py), Australia complete: PUT /timezone Australia/Melbourne stores the string that name stands for and the name (the Preferences screen has the same picker, above), so the screen can say Melbourne (the string alone could not: Melbourne, Sydney, Hobart and Canberra are one string). A name from the table says the zone, so a release that carries a government's new rules moves the boards that chose that city and no others. A name that is not in the table is a 400, as is a string that is not a zone. A string that is not a zone, or one with daylight saving and no rules (which the board's C library would give its own for, differently from the next one's), is refused with a 400 that says so; one the grammar accepts is then read by the board's own C library, on a day in January and a day in July, and refused with a 409 if the library did not read it as written (a library that cannot parse a string falls back to UTC without saying so). With none set the time is UTC.

    A screen or a page never shows the string. It is the board's way of keeping a zone and the routes' way of reporting it (GET /timezone, GET /status's zone and the Preferences entry's value, for tools); a person reads the place (iclforge::wallclock::zone_readable, host tested). The name they chose is the place. A zone set with a string and no name (through the page before it had a picker, or by hand) is read as the place the table holds for the string: an exact match, and where several places share it (Melbourne, Sydney, Hobart and Canberra are one) the most populous, which tools/hearth/gen_time_zones.py writes down as PREFERRED (51 strings are shared; the table says which place each means, in 102 bytes of flash), so a board set to AEST-10AEDT,M10.1.0,M4.1.0/3 reads Melbourne (Australia) on the Preferences row, in the picker (which opens at Australia with Melbourne marked) and on the page, though no name was stored. A zone in no table reads as its offset and as the person's own: UTC+09:45 (custom), UTC+09:30 (custom, with daylight saving). The guess is display only and is not stored: it would turn a guess at which of four cities was meant into a choice. GET /status's time has the place (zone_place: a name in the table, or none) and the words (zone_label: Melbourne (Australia), UTC, UTC+09:45 (custom)), which the page shows in Now and under the time zone. The page's field for a zone not in the list is never filled in with the board's string; Save with nothing typed asks for a choice and sends nothing (UTC is Other, then UTC).

    The grammar and the words (libs/device/include/iclforge/wall_clock.hpp) are held to the letter by the host tests (test_wall_clock.cpp: the zones and the servers taken and the ones refused, the offsets, the dates and the words; test_time_zones.cpp: every string in the table taken by the board's reader, Australia complete, and Melbourne's changeovers, with 15 other zones', at tzdata's own instants), and the device page's stand-in is held to the same lists (apps/demos/wasm/tests/device-ui/contract.spec.js). The generator holds every string to tzdata itself (gen_time_zones.py --verify: 4,500 changes from 2026 to 2036, no disagreement).

    Wired, and Wi-Fi¶

    The kit's RJ45 port (the chip's own Ethernet MAC and an IP101GR PHY on RMII, 10/100 Mbit/s) is a network of the sink as the Wi-Fi is, so the kit can sit on a PoE splitter with no USB cable: the splitter's Ethernet leg into the RJ45, its USB-C leg into the kit's power input (not the connector marked JTAG, which is the console and the flashing port). The image is updated over the same network, the way Sink firmware describes, and everything that answers on Wi-Fi answers on wired: the control surface and the page on port 80, GET /log, PUT /firmware, mDNS (_sendspin._tcp on 8928 and hearth-<mac tail>.local), the Sendspin player and its pairing, and the idle screen, which shows the address and then Wired or Wi-Fi <network>.

    Which one it uses. The board takes an address on whichever has a link, by DHCP, under the name it is called (hearth-<mac tail>). With auto, the default, it uses wired while wired holds an address and the Wi-Fi when only that does, and goes back to wired when it returns, with no restart. Both are up when the Ethernet cable is in and a Wi-Fi network is stored, the wired interface with the higher route priority (200 against the Wi-Fi's 100; ESP-IDF's own number for Ethernet, 50, would have put a board with both on Wi-Fi). A board with the Ethernet cable in and no Wi-Fi network stored comes up on wired alone and needs nothing else. The setting, network preference, chooses what the board does:

    Preference Wired The Wi-Fi station
    auto used while it holds an address joined as the way out if wired goes; used when only it holds an address
    ethernet the only network switched off: no radio, nothing joined
    wifi switched off the only network

    It is stored in the board's NVS like its name and its Wi-Fi network, so it survives a restart and an update, and it is changed over REST, in the style of PUT /name:

    curl http://192.168.1.251/network-preference                     # auto
curl -X PUT -d ethernet http://192.168.1.251/network-preference  # ok; takes effect in a moment, ...

    A pinned preference leaves the board with one network and nothing to fall back to, so a change made while the board runs is checked before it is kept. ethernet brings wired up first (it waits for a link and an address) and only then stores the preference and switches the Wi-Fi off; wifi joins the stored network first and only then stores it and stops wired. A change whose interface does not come up is not kept: the preference stays what it was, the other interface stays on, and the console says so (network: no wired link answered, so the WiFi station is not switched off). The reply (200) is sent before any of this, so it comes back over the interface the change may switch off; read GET /network-preference a few seconds later to see what was kept. PUT /network-preference wifi with no Wi-Fi network stored is refused (409). Improv over USB and PUT /network still store a Wi-Fi network, and a board on wired alone reports Improv's ready state (it has no Wi-Fi), so a client still offers its Wi-Fi form; the Wi-Fi it is given joins while wired stays in use.

    What GET /status says. The network object keeps its four fields (kind, ssid, rssi_dbm, address), which now describe the interface in use (kind is "ethernet" or "wifi", and while none holds an address it is the one the board is waiting on), and adds preference and interfaces: how each interface stands, and never the passphrase. On a board with one network preference and interfaces are null.

    "network": {
  "kind": "ethernet", "ssid": "", "rssi_dbm": null, "address": "192.168.1.163",
  "preference": "auto",
  "interfaces": {
    "ethernet": {"enabled": true, "link": true, "address": "192.168.1.163",
                 "mac": "e8:f6:0a:e7:53:f9", "speed_mbps": 100, "full_duplex": true},
    "wifi":     {"enabled": true, "link": true, "address": "192.168.1.251",
                 "mac": "98:88:e0:72:59:b0", "configured": true, "ssid": "...", "rssi_dbm": -40}
  }
}

    enabled is "not switched off by the preference", link is a partner on the Ethernet port (wired) or an association with the access point (Wi-Fi), configured says a Wi-Fi network is stored, and mac is the interface's own address, for a DHCP reservation (the wired one is the chip's base MAC, the one the board's name is made from; the Wi-Fi's is the C6's). A diagnostics view or a control for the preference reads GET /status and GET and PUT /network-preference and nothing else.

    Moving a kit to PoE

    On a PoE splitter the kit has no USB console and no USB flashing, so a bad image can only be rescued by the update's own rollback (an image that does not reach the network, the HTTP server and the Sendspin player inside five minutes goes back to the one before it, and so does one that panics on its first boot; both were tried on the kit) or by putting the USB cable back. Keep the Wi-Fi network stored, so the board has a way out if wired goes. See What was tested for what was and was not tried.

    What was tested on wired¶

    On the v3.2 kit, 2026-10-20, with the owner's Ethernet cable to their router (100 Mbit/s, full duplex), the Wi-Fi and the USB cable on as well. Details and the numbers: the plan.

    • The update path, before wired: ten ota.py push runs (eight accepted, an image that never becomes healthy rolled back at its 300 s deadline, and one that panics rolled back by the bootloader in about 25 s), a PUT rollback, eight POST /restart and four rounds of a USB flash followed 20 s later by an update: the C6 link was back each time, 7 to 10 s after the restart.
    • The PHY answers on the default pins at SMI address 1 with the IP101's ID, and negotiates 100 Mbit/s full duplex.
    • Boot: with the Ethernet cable in, it answers on wired 10 s after reset and the Wi-Fi joins behind it at 14 s; with no Ethernet cable it waits 4 s for a link and is on Wi-Fi at about 16 s; with the cable in and no Wi-Fi network stored it comes up on wired alone, with the page, mDNS and the Sendspin player, and Improv over USB then gives it a Wi-Fi network that joins while wired stays in use.
    • An Ethernet cable put in while the board runs is in use within about 6 s, with no restart. A cable pulled (four times, by the owner, three with the board idle and one in the middle of a play) made the Wi-Fi the interface in use within half a second, with no restart, and wired returned 4 to 5 s after the cable was put back. A play cut by the pull ended there (926 bursts, no underrun until it) and the board was idle and answering; the next play ran in full.
    • Everything that answers on Wi-Fi answers on wired: the page, /api, /hardware, GET /log, PUT /firmware (an update over wired restarted the board, which answered on wired 10 s later and passed its trial), mDNS (hearth-<mac tail>.local has an A record for each address) and Sendspin, which played four 30 s AC-3 streams through wired, 938 of 938 bursts each with no underrun (one with a late burst and a resync at its start).
    • The preference set to each of the three words with the Ethernet cable in did what the table says, and survived a restart; ethernet with no cable in was not kept and left the Wi-Fi on throughout. With ethernet set (the Wi-Fi radio off), an update sent to the wired address restarted the board, which came back on wired alone and passed its trial: the PoE case with the radio off.
    • Bad images on wired: an image that panics on its trial and one that never reports healthy, both built with wired, pushed to the wired address, were rolled back (about 25 s, and at the 300 s deadline) and the board returned on wired.
    • Cost: the image grew by 51 KB (2,518,416 bytes) and the internal heap at boot is 15 to 25 KB smaller (about 281 KB free, largest block about 256 KB).

    Not tested: a PoE splitter, 10 Mbit/s or half duplex, a static address, IPv6 and a long soak. A board whose DHCP server is slow gives wired 15 s at boot before the Wi-Fi is joined as well (wired goes on asking). A kit on PoE should keep its Wi-Fi network stored, so that it has a way out if wired goes, and the first move to PoE should be done with the USB cable at hand.

    Name, restart and reset¶

    The routes that name, restart and reset a board are the same on every Hearth board (Name, restart and reset has the rules and the table of what a rename changes); what the kit adds is below.

    • A restart resets the whole system, not the CPU alone, through the RTC watchdog (system_reset.cpp), because a CPU reset sometimes left the radio on the ESP32-C6 with a dead link. The restart a person asks for - POST /restart, and the restart that Restart and the factory reset end in - all go through it, a second slower than a CPU reset, with the USB console's port gone for a second or two.
    • A factory reset leaves the kit reachable on wired. Wired needs nothing stored: the kit asks the router for an address again, and has no Wi-Fi network until you give it one (the page's Wi-Fi network field, or Improv over the USB-C marked JTAG). The same reset on a board without an Ethernet cable plugged in leaves it with no network at all. Whether wired has a link is what the confirming step reads, and it says which of the two the board is in.
    • The radio's own copy of the network. ESP-IDF's Wi-Fi driver keeps the SSID and passphrase it is given in NVS, and on the kit the driver is the ESP32-C6's. The factory reset asks the radio to forget them (esp_wifi_restore) when the Wi-Fi was up this run; it cannot reach a radio that was never started. Not yet checked on the kit.
    • Restart is refused for about half a minute after an update: the new image is on trial, and a restart then goes back to the image before it.

    Memory and time¶

    The screen's own memory is in PSRAM, none in the internal RAM the AC-4 decoder leaves 1 to 11 KB of: the frame buffer (1.2 MB at 16 bits), two render buffers (80 KB each), LVGL's heap and glyph caches, the copies of the lines it shows (the rows, a file's plain text and the title are held in LVGL's heap, not ESP-IDF's, which would try internal RAM first for anything up to 16 KB), and the task's stack. The task runs at priority 2, below the decoder and the I2S writer.

    Measured on the v1.3 board with no panel (a FireBeetle 2 at 360 MHz, a synthetic karaoke AC-3 programme decoded to 2.0 over Wi-Fi for 60 s, the screen filling a looping song at 30 fps):

    Screen idle Screen filling
    Underruns, late, dropped chunks 0, 0, 0 0, 0, 0
    A burst's decode and render, mean 4.50 ms 4.58 ms (+1.6 %)
    A burst, worst 5.7 to 6.0 ms 5.8 to 6.2 ms
    Refreshes a minute 60 about 960, 1.7 ms each (2.7 % of one core)
    A line's rows rebuilt 28 to 48 ms, once a line

    One of five runs with the screen filling broke some 15 to 20 seconds in (underruns and resyncs, then every chunk late until the stream ended); the three that followed it on the same image did not, and a run with the screen idle had a smaller disturbance of the same kind. The cause was not found, and the screen is neither shown to be it nor shown not to be (the plan's Measured section). Not in these numbers: the DSI engine's own reads of PSRAM (74 MB/s at 60 Hz, on the bus the decoder fetches its code from), and the AC-4 5.1 streams that fill a frame. The screen task's refresh took 2.3 ms on average over that play (1.6 ms for the demo song alone), a line rebuild up to 48.9 ms (the plan's 30 ms is not met), and the audio had no late chunk beside it.
    • The scan-out and the drawing split differently by stream. On the light streams most of the move while the song fills is the drawing (4.6% and 11.7% on panel-null, which draws and does not scan out), and on AC-4 5.1 the song filling adds almost nothing (0.1%) to a 10% that is already there with the screen idle. 5% was the plan's pass; the light streams exceed it while the song fills, by 0.3 to 0.65% of a frame, and AC-4 5.1 exceeds it all the time.
    • AC-4 5.1 on the kit. The decoder's PCM is the v1.3 board's, bit for bit. A Sendspin burst takes 74% of the 42.7 ms frame on the glass at layout 9.1.6 (66% with no panel); at 5.1 it takes 65% on the glass (27.65 ms, 25.27 ms with no scan-out, 26.91 ms with the glass at 40 Hz). The scan-out costs 2.4 ms a burst at 60 Hz, a linear 0.04 ms a hertz, and no AXI priority setting changes it. The least free internal heap is 11 to 15 KB (the v1.3 board: 27.7 KB; the decoder takes whatever is left, and the TDM DMA queue left 124 KB less at 5.1 before PR #1607). A bench-only p4-rev3-ac4 profile is worth adding to hold these rows; whether the kit should ship AC-4 is the owner's call.
    • The TDM DMA queue holds the layout's channels. The sink clocks sixteen slots whatever the layout and, until PR #1607, enabled all of them, so its 64 ms queue was 196,608 bytes of internal RAM at every layout from three channels up (24,576 at 2.0); it is now 4 bytes a frame for each channel, 73,728 at 5.1. A 5.1 karaoke play left 46,139 bytes of internal heap at its least and leaves 171,151.

    Developing the screen without a panel¶

    Two tools, neither needing the kit.

    On a computer. firmware/hearth-sink/preview builds the firmware's lyric screen against LVGL's software renderer and writes one picture per screen above, and the developer view's (opened with three taps on the name, on fixture data), in seconds:

    cmake -S firmware/hearth-sink/preview -B build-preview -DLVGL_DIR=<firmware/hearth-sink/managed_components/lvgl__lvgl>
cmake --build build-preview
build-preview/panel_preview <output directory>

    managed_components/lvgl__lvgl appears after any P4 build of firmware/hearth-sink. It reads the example songs in examples/karaoke and writes 24-bit BMP files. After the screens above it draws Adjust and Preferences at 1024 by 600, 800 by 480, 480 by 320 and 320 by 240, for the kit and for a board whose screen has no touch (iclforge/board_variants.hpp), and checks them: it exits 3 when text is clipped, a control is smaller than a fingertip or sticks out of its sheet, a tap does nothing, or a greyed choice answers one.

    On a P4 with no panel (sdkconfig.panel-null): LVGL draws into a frame buffer in PSRAM, and a second small HTTP server (port 8081, ICLFORGE_EXAMPLE_PANEL_CAPTURE_PORT, 0 for none) serves it. That server has no authentication (anyone on the network can read the screen and press its buttons), so a released image does not start it: the port is 0 in the release profiles (p4-rev3 locks it) and in the Kconfig default, and package_firmware.py refuses a build with any other. An image that is run on a bench turns it on with the panel-capture overlay (sink_profile.py defaults p4-rev3 --overlay panel-capture), and panel-null has it on because that build exists to be read back:

    Request Answer
    GET /panel.bmp the picture, as a 24-bit BMP
    GET /panel what drawing has cost: refreshes, pixels, microseconds a refresh, the longest rebuild, late cycles, the task's stack left; which no-lyrics view is chosen and whether the screen is dimmed; the time of day's state (clock_enabled, clock_asking, clock_synced and clock_syncs); and whether the developer view is open (dev_view) and what its refreshes cost
    POST /panel/tap?x=..&y=.. a tap, for a board with no touch controller (or to try one: three of them within 600 ms at x=100&y=568 open the developer view, and a tap on it half a second later closes it)

    The same screen code runs on both and on the glass; what the computer and the board with no panel leave out is the glass itself.
