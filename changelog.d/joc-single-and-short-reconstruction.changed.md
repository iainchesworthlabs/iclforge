**Decoding Atmos objects on a float decoder (the ESP32-S3 and ESP32-P4 images) and on short syncframes is faster.** The object reconstruction's mixing step ran the interpolation rule through a `switch` for every band of every channel (single precision) or for every coefficient with a division apiece (short frames, always double). The rule is now picked once per object and timeslot and each rule is a loop of its own: the single-precision path on the SIMD seam's four-lane float, its previous-frame coefficients read once an object and the sum over the five bed channels taken through the band mapping, the short-frame path on the two-lane double rows `reconstruct_qmf` already uses. Every operation keeps its type and order (a division stays a division, no reciprocal, no fused multiply-add, floats stay float), so every decoded sample is unchanged. MSVC Release on a Ryzen 9 5950X pinned to one logical processor, median of 7, two base/new pairs, `-DICLFORGE_DECODE_SCALAR=float` build: `joc_reconstruct_qmf_single_4obj` 84,751/63,515 and 84,756/64,149 ns (-25.1 %, -24.3 %), `joc_reconstruct_qmf_single_12obj` -32.6 %, -32.7 %, `joc_reconstruct_qmf_short_4obj` -26.0 %, -23.2 %, `joc_reconstruct_qmf_short_12obj` -35.3 %, -33.6 %, `atmos_4obj_decode` 0.1677/0.1403 and 0.1647/0.1408 ms a frame (-16.3 %, -14.5 %). The board gain is not measured. The decoded and exported-object WAVs of eleven streams (four of them short-frame) are byte-identical to the previous build's in both the float and the double build.
