**The AC-4 encoder codes a frame's spectrum in about half the time, byte for byte the same stream.** `libs/ac4/src/encoder/asf/coder.cpp` priced every band once per codebook, searched the section lengths with a running sum per candidate, and took `std::pow(x, 0.75)` of every line. It now prices the codebooks that share a shape (Tables A.2 to A.12 pair them) in one walk of the band, searches the sections over per-codebook prefix sums and a header-length table in the same candidate order (so the same candidate wins every tie), and quantises with `sqrt(y * sqrt(y))`, falling back to `std::pow` whenever the result is within 1e-9 of an integer boundary, so each line quantises to the integer it always did. `iclforge-bench` on a Ryzen 9 5950X, MSVC Release, median ms per frame of 7 pinned runs, two base/new pairs each: `ac4_51_encode` 3.833 to 2.167 and 3.844 to 2.133 (about 44 % less), `ac4_stereo_encode` 2.000 to 1.002 and 2.004 to 0.988 (about 50 % less). The streams hash exactly as before (`ref51_x40.wav` at 128, 256 and 640 kbit/s; `reference_stereo.wav` and `ref51_x10.wav` at seven other rates compared byte for byte). A new test holds the coder to a transcription of the old per-codebook pricing and section search. Not measured on Linux, macOS or Arm.
