**The AC-4 encoder makes about a tenth of the heap allocations it did and codes a 5.1 frame in about 36 % less time, byte for byte the same stream.** The rate loop built a whole coded track (a vector of vectors for each of the lines, offsets, codebooks, scale factors, sections and noise levels) every time it needed only a track's bit count, about nine times a frame in each of the frame's channels; the audio writers kept a syntax record for every field though nothing reads them without a syntax trace; and the spectrum, layout, scale-factor and threshold vectors were rebuilt each frame. `TrackCoder` (`libs/ac4/src/encoder/asf/coder.cpp`) now counts the bits of one band at a time without keeping the track, and writes the track it settles on into storage it already holds; the sum of root magnitudes the scale factors take of a band's lines is worked out once a frame instead of at every step; a band whose largest line is under 0.45 of a quantiser step (21 % of the lines in the stereo benchmark, 40 % in 5.1) is priced as the zeros it is without being quantised; the writers keep their records only when `trace` is set; and `SubstreamCoder` keeps the per-frame vectors it fills. `iclforge-membench` per frame, MSVC Release on a Ryzen 9 5950X: `ac4_51_encode` 1,351.8 allocations and 2,047,180 bytes to 137.0 and 121,292 (-90 %, -94 %), `ac4_stereo_encode` 574.4 and 975,616 to 90.7 and 43,976 (-84 %, -95 %); the live bytes it grows by over 199 frames stay within 4 % of what they were (141,054 against 136,921 and 53,492 against 51,485). `iclforge-bench`, median ms per frame of 7 pinned runs, two base/new pairs each: `ac4_51_encode` 2.143 to 1.390 and 2.167 to 1.341, `ac4_stereo_encode` 1.014 to 0.812 and 1.016 to 0.765; `forge ac4-encode` of `ref51_x40.wav` at 256 kbit/s takes 7.9 s to 5.7 s (median of 5). The streams hash exactly as before (`ref51_x40.wav` at 128, 256 and 640 kbit/s, and 24 more encodes of stereo, 5.1 and object inputs compared byte for byte). New tests hold the bit count and the refilled track to `code_track()` over random frames. Not measured on Linux, macOS or Arm.
