**The AC-3 and E-AC-3 encoders (and the Atmos encoder through E-AC-3) choose the coded bandwidth with a cheaper scan, and write the same bytes.** The scan used to convert every coefficient of every block to fixed point and to an exponent only to keep the smallest exponent per bin, which was 15 % of an AC-3 encode and 9 % of an E-AC-3 encode in a sampling profile. It now keeps the largest magnitude per bin and converts each bin once per frame, which gives the same exponent for every input because the exponent never rises as the magnitude grows (NaN, whose conversion the language leaves undefined, is handled by the same two calls as before). On an AMD Ryzen 9 5950X with MSVC, pinned to one core, medians of 7 runs, two base/new pairs: `plain_51_fast_mdct` 0.3727 to 0.3044 ms per frame and 0.3774 to 0.3030 (18 % and 20 % less), `eac3_51_auto` 0.4815 to 0.4102 and 0.4706 to 0.4055 (15 % and 14 % less), `eac3_stereo_auto` 6 % and 5 % less, `atmos_4obj_qmf_fast_mdct` 10 % and 8 % less. 26 encodes across AC-3, E-AC-3 and Atmos at a range of bit rates and layouts are byte for byte identical to the previous build. New library functions `accumulate_peak_scan` and `peak_scan_exponents` (with `PeakScan`) carry the scan; `accumulate_peak_exponents` is unchanged.
