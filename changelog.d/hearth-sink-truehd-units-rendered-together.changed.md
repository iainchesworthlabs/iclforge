**An ESP32 Hearth sink renders the TrueHD access units of a chunk together.** A TrueHD access unit is 40 samples at 48 kHz, and the sink ran its whole block path (layout renderer, routing, trims, delays, meter, hand-over to the output) once for each: 1,200 times a second, where AC-3 runs it 187.5 times. The units of a chunk that share a layout now go through it in blocks of up to 240 samples, 200 times a second, and on a part with PSRAM the chunk is decoded before its first block is rendered. The samples, play times and meter levels are the same: 39 stream-and-layout runs of the former player against this one on the host were equal in every sample. On an ESP32-S3 a 20 ms chunk of 2.0 at 24 bit took 10.87 ms where it took 11.67 (one play of each, 1,040 chunks). It does not make 5.1 play on the S3, where the decoder alone takes 20.3 ms of the 20 ms. Two settings, `CONFIG_ICLFORGE_EXAMPLE_SENDSPIN_TRUEHD_BLOCK_SAMPLES` and `CONFIG_ICLFORGE_EXAMPLE_SENDSPIN_TRUEHD_DECODE_AHEAD`, set to 0 give the former path. Not run on a P4 or a C6 board. The sink's player now has host tests (`firmware/esp-idf/iclforge-host-tests`, option `ICLFORGE_BUILD_FIRMWARE_HOST_TESTS`, off by default and built by no CI leg).
