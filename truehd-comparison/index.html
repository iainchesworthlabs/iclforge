    TrueHD encoder and decoder against FFmpeg and DEE¶

    Measured on 2026-10-31, on one Windows machine, with the tools named at the end

    Every number on this page comes from a script in the tree that anyone can run again, and every claim names the table that carries it. Where forge is not better, the page says so. The scripts are compare_truehd_encoders.py (stream size) and compare_truehd_decoders.py (decoder speed and what each decoder does with a damaged stream); the library's own workloads are iclforge-bench, iclforge-perf and iclforge-membench (Performance trend).

    TrueHD is lossless, so an encoder has no quality to race: the stream either decodes to the source or it does not, and what is left to compare is size. A stream counts here only if it decodes to the source. That is checked for forge's by forge's own decoder and by FFmpeg's strict decode, and for FFmpeg's and DEE's by FFmpeg's strict decode; a stream that does not is shown with its bytes, marked *, and left out of the ratios and totals.

    Encoder size¶

    Method. forge truehd-encode (restart interval 128 units, FIR order up to 8, pair matrices, its own verification on) and FFmpeg's encoder (-c:a truehd -strict -2) write a stream of each source. DEE's stream of the same source is read from a gold set when there is one: the 13 legs committed in testdata/truehd-dee (made on 2026-10-29 with DEE 6.5.4, whose licence ends on 2026-11-06) are the default, and the full set stays on the owner's machine (--set). DEE's streams are test inputs: their bytes are counted and forge probe prints their summary fields, nothing else about them is read. The second group of rows is cut from testdata/audio (--programme). The tables are bytes; the ratio is forge's bytes over the other stream's, so below 1 is smaller.

    Corpus, and what it is not. The 13 sources are 0.08 to 0.25 s. The programme rows are 1.5 to 10 s of the repository's own material: a Mendelssohn recording and a spoken-word recording (both 16 bit, 48 kHz), and the 5.1 reference programme (16 bit, 2.5 s long, of which the rows use 1.5 s). There is no 24-bit programme material in the repository: the rows marked 24 bits put 16-bit samples in a 24-bit container, which the encoder sends as an output shift, so they come out the same size as the 16-bit rows. The 7.1 rows are the 5.1 programme with its first two channels repeated as the rear surrounds, not 7.1 content, and the 96 and 192 kHz rows are the 48 kHz recordings resampled by FFmpeg. Real 24-bit, 7.1 and film material is below, from the owner's discs and local only; speech and music at 24 bit are still ERRATA question 92.

    On the 5.1 film forge is 0.95 of FFmpeg's size, as on the programme rows, and on the 96 kHz stereo and 5.1 0.84 to 0.94. On the concert, real full-band music, forge is 0.93 to 0.95 of FFmpeg's size at 48 kHz (stereo, 5.1), 0.97 at 96 kHz and 1.02 and 1.04 at 192 kHz: the concert is louder, full-band music where the programme rows are the repository's own recordings, and the 192 kHz loss is the specification's order-4 limit (below, ERRATA question 96). FFmpeg's 7.1 streams do not decode to the source, so they have no ratio. A 7.1 file with the standard channel mask lists BL BR before SL SR, TrueHD has Ls Rs before Lrs Rrs, and forge decode writes no mask: its output has the last four channels swapped in pairs, which the harness expects and FFmpeg's decode, which writes the mask's order, does not show.

    What it shows.

    • 5.1 music, the row that was worst: 101,314 bytes before, 56,672 now, against DEE's 58,688 and FFmpeg's 63,254 (0.966 and 0.896). The cause was one output shift and quantiser step for all six channels: the source's L, R, Ls and Rs are 16-bit material (7 or 8 clear low bits) and its C and LFE use all 24 bits, so the single step sent the low 8 bits of four channels for nothing. The encoder now sends each channel's own shift and step (ERRATA entry 90); a test fails if it does not (each channel sends its own clear low bits..., mutation-checked), and the pull-request flow fails a change that makes any of the 13 sources larger than FFmpeg's or DEE's stream of it.
    • On the 13 committed sources forge is smaller than FFmpeg's encoder on every row where FFmpeg's stream is the source (0.507 to 0.932) and smaller than DEE's on every row where DEE's is (0.553 to 0.997; 0.734 over the 11 rows). Those totals are led by the length-test and tone rows, where forge is 30 to 50 per cent smaller; on the two real 48 kHz music sources forge is 1 and 3.4 per cent smaller than DEE and 17.5 and 10.4 per cent smaller than FFmpeg. The DEE rows at 0.997 and 0.990 are ties, not wins.
    • On the programme material forge is 0.85 to 0.96 of FFmpeg's size at 44.1 to 96 kHz (0.954 over all 22 rows FFmpeg's stream is the source of, the 13 sources included).
    • At 192 kHz forge is larger than FFmpeg: 1.064 stereo and 1.149 for 5.1. The specification limits the filter order there to 4 and FFmpeg's encoder writes order 7 (ERRATA entry 35). FFmpeg 8.0.1's strict decode refused forge's 192 kHz streams of 3 or more channels while they had one substream, as the text of the specification has it; its own encoder writes two, and so does forge now, with FFmpeg's decode of the 5.1 row exact (ERRATA entry 90, provisional until the owner confirms).
    • DEE's 96 kHz streams are not the source: FFmpeg's strict decode of them differs from it (about -47 dB, ERRATA entry 50), so their sizes are not a size for the same audio and are not compared. Forge is 0.932 of FFmpeg's size on the 96 kHz music, and the gap to DEE's smaller figure is not a gap in coding.
    • FFmpeg's 8-channel streams are not the source either (* on the 7.1 and 8-channel rows), so FFmpeg has no 7.1 size to compare with.
    • What was tried and is not in the encoder. A least-squares (covariance) FIR candidate beside the windowed autocorrelation one changed the 96 kHz music by 0.04 per cent and was dropped. On that source a long filter (order 32) needs about 11 per cent fewer bits than order 8 by an estimate with exact coefficients, which is the case of music resampled up by FFmpeg. Measured on real recordings, with the coefficients quantised as the format asks and the recursion run in integers (tools/checks/estimate_truehd_iir_gain.py, 13 files), the format's IIR filter next to a FIR of order 8 is 99.9 to 100.4 per cent of order 8's residual bits, and 99.7 to 100.0 per cent if the better of the two is taken for each segment; it saves 3.7 per cent at 96 kHz and 11 per cent at 192 kHz only on the resampled test signal. It is not written (ERRATA question 91).
    • Against a retail encoder's stream of the same audio. The 192 kHz and 48 kHz 5.1 TrueHD tracks of an animated film's UHD Blu-ray were decoded and re-encoded by forge: a unit of forge's stream is 0.990 of the retail unit's size at 192 kHz and 0.995 at 48 kHz (bytes per access unit, the two excerpts covering slightly different spans), exact in forge's decoder and in FFmpeg's strict decode; on 30 s excerpts forge is 0.997 and 1.010 of FFmpeg's encoder's size at 192 kHz and 0.90 at 48 kHz. Both retail tracks have the layout forge writes at these rates, two substreams of 2 and 6 channels; their substream 1 carries restart sync 0x31EB, which forge now writes too (ERRATA entry 97).

    What it shows, and what it does not.

    • Recovery is the same. With the failure counted and the decode going on, forge and FFmpeg recover the same frames at the next restart in every case (the same exact column), including after a lost unit, junk between units and a cut stream. Forge is not better at resynchronising and the page does not say it is.
    • A flipped bit in a payload is where they differ. FFmpeg warns that the substream's parity, checksum and lossless check failed and writes 2,545 wrong frames with exit status 0, as it runs and with the strict flags. Forge as it runs stops at that unit and exits 2. A library test flips 48 bits across two units of a real stream and a strict decode refuses all 48 (one bit flipped in the payload of any unit..., mutation-checked: switching the parity and CRC checks off fails all 48). Forge lenient does what FFmpeg does, and counts the failure.
    • Exit status. Forge as it runs refuses every damage except the junk before the first unit, which all four skip. FFmpeg as it runs exits 0 on all of them, and with the strict flags it fails on a dropped or zeroed unit only. Whether stopping is better than writing what can be read is the caller's choice, and forge offers both.

    What is not measured¶

    • No 24-bit programme material and no real 7.1 or film material (above).
    • DEE's speed: it is a licensed binary on another machine; the question is ERRATA question 94.
    • Memory beyond iclforge-membench's per-frame counts; the TrueHD rows are in Performance trend.
    • Windows, macOS, ARM and the small parts: CI legs and the ESP32 phase of the plan, not this machine.
    • The tiers of the arithmetic audit: the decoder is exact integer arithmetic and has one tier; the encoder's analysis is double, and the one AVX2 kernel (the bit count) has its own bit-exactness test. No float, fixed-point or further SIMD tier was written here.

    Run it again¶

    python tools/checks/compare_truehd_encoders.py --forge build/bin/forge --programme
python tools/checks/compare_truehd_encoders.py --forge build/bin/forge --set D:/ac3bld/dee-gold   # the full set, on the owner's machine
python tools/checks/compare_truehd_decoders.py --forge build/bin/forge

    Tools: forge 0.10.0-beta.1+5866; FFmpeg 8.0.1 (gyan.dev full build, the version .github/workflows/_ci-core.yml pins); DEE 6.5.4 for the committed legs; Windows 11, MSVC 14.51 Release, AMD Ryzen 9 5950X (16 cores).
