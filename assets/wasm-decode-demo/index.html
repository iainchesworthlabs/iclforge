ICL Forge - WASM decode demo

A real AC-3/E-AC-3 decoder, compiled to WebAssembly and running entirely in this tab: no server-side decode, no upload. Load the bundled Atmos-in-DD+ stream below, or drop in your own .ec3/.ac3 elementary stream.

This is a karaoke stream (Annex C of ETSI TS 102 366): L and R, a guide melody where a centre would be, and one or two vocals where the surrounds would be. A stereo fold is already Annex C's 2/0 reproduction, with the vocals at the level the programme set. A karaoke-capable decoder lets the listener choose which vocals are heard; choosing one decodes the stream again with it. The reproduction is the library's, the one forge decode karaoke=v1 writes.

Through open speakers the microphone hears the backing as well as you, and a score reads as nearly perfect for no singing, so the score is shown only when you say you are wearing headphones. The browser's own echo cancellation is on. It counts how much of the tune's time your pitch was within a semitone of it, in any octave: pitch only, not timing, words or feeling. The tracker, tune reader and scorer are the project's own library (libs/pitch), compiled into this page.

Real decoded Atmos object positions (OAMD) - top-down and side-on, same layout as the desktop GUI's Objects tab. Dot size is that object's own real audio level (JOC).

Solo an object to hear its own real isolated audio - not its panned slice of the bed.

Solid ring: ear-level channels. Dashed ring: ceiling channels (only lights up for a 7.1.4-class stream). Drag the slider above to scrub. Dot size/glow and the yellow arrow are real per-channel bed energy from the decode.

Everything on this page is real: the decode, the audio, the bed's per-channel energy, and - for a stream with objects - each object's real decoded position (OAMD, iclforge#168) and its own real isolated audio (JOC, iclforge#169). Nothing here is a canned animation or a re-panned approximation.

Realtime push-decode (AudioWorklet)

The same bundled stream, fed one access unit at a time through the bundled iclforge-wasm-decoder push-frame API into an AudioWorkletNode. Decoding happens in a Worker; a small ring-buffer drain runs on the audio thread. Needs cross-origin isolation (SharedArrayBuffer) - see docs/platforms/wasm.md if this button stays disabled.
